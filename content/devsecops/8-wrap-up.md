Budget: 7 minutes, outside the 75-minute core. Stop new troubleshooting at event minute 80 and begin closing at minute 83.

## Why it matters

A useful workshop record separates controls you tested yourself from demonstrations you watched and work that remains unfinished.

## Try it

1. Complete your [evidence checklist](evidence.md). Mark code, dependencies, and secret protection as live-complete only when you have the required results.
2. Compare your opening delivery sketch with the process you examined. Identify where a change is prevented, detected, reviewed, and approved.
3. Leave `exercise/shelter-change` open for take-home. Keep dependency and secret training branches unmerged. Save run links before their artifacts expire.
4. Open [Resume](take-home/0-resume.md) and choose your state: completed live work, partial work, or a fresh copy.
5. Write one improvement to discuss with your team and use the remaining time for questions.

## Stop the codespace safely

Save edits and review `git status --short` in the Codespaces terminal. Commit and push intended safe work to its exercise branch, then verify it on GitHub; a saved file alone is not a pushed commit. Do not publish a rejected secret fixture as a backup.

Use **Codespaces: Stop Codespace** or **Stop codespace** for this space at [github.com/codespaces](https://github.com/codespaces). Closing the tab does not stop compute. Stopped storage still counts toward usage; resume this same space for take-home work. Delete it only after preserving required work and evidence, since deletion also removes the sibling kit. Follow [Step 0's lifecycle guidance](0-setup.md#010-stop-and-reuse-the-same-codespace).

## Checkpoint

Keep your evidence record and resume point. Label pending scans, recorded demonstrations, and unavailable fixtures accurately when the event ends.
